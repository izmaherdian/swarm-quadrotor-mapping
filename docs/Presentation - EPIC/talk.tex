\kick{Problem}{}
\begin{frame}{The question is not whether a swarm is faster, but whether it still finishes}
  \mins{6}%
  \begin{itemize}\bulletgap
    \item Non-convex region --- notches and arms break a simple back-and-forth sweep
    \item Wind --- mean \WindMeanMag\,m/s, gusts up to \WindMaxH\,m/s
    \item Obstacles not known in advance, found in flight by LiDAR
    \item Two of seven quadrotors lost mid-mission
  \end{itemize}
  \vfill
  \begin{callout}{All four at once}
    Not one at a time --- the swarm has to keep mapping while every one of
    these is happening.
  \end{callout}
  \narasi{So what's the problem? To map an area, every spot has to pass under a sensor. More drones make it faster, but things can go wrong. In our test, four things go wrong at the same time. The area is non-convex. The wind is strong, about \WindMeanMag\space metres per second, with gusts up to \WindMaxH. The obstacles are not known in advance. And two of the seven drones stop working.}
    {Jadi apa masalahnya? Untuk memetakan area, setiap titik harus terlewati sensor. Lebih banyak drone membuatnya lebih cepat, tetapi banyak hal bisa salah. Dalam pengujian kami, empat hal salah sekaligus. Areanya non-konveks. Anginnya kencang, sekitar \WindMeanMag\space meter per detik, dengan hembusan sampai \WindMaxH. Rintangannya tidak diketahui sebelumnya. Dan dua dari tujuh drone berhenti bekerja.}
    {Read the four bullets as you name them.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Four contributions}
  \mins{4}%
  \begin{itemize}\bulletgap
    \item Lanes that follow each cell's shape, so tapered corners get swept
    \item Lane reallocation when an agent is lost --- merge, re-plan, hand over
    \item A safety filter whose speed limit comes from the real tilt limit
    \item \lqr{} vs \hinf{} on an identical structure --- only the gains differ
  \end{itemize}
  \narasi{We bring four ideas. Lanes that follow the shape of each cell. Reallocating lanes when a drone is lost. A safety filter based on the real actuator limits. And a fair comparison of two controllers, PID-LQR and PID H-infinity.}
    {Kami membawa empat ide. Lajur yang mengikuti bentuk tiap sel. Realokasi lajur saat sebuah drone hilang. Filter keselamatan yang berdasar pada batas aktuator sebenarnya. Dan perbandingan yang adil antara dua pengendali, PID-LQR dan PID H-infinity.}
    {One breath per bullet.}
\end{frame}

    {Sistemnya punya tiga lapis, dan tiap lapis memberikan setpoint ke lapis berikutnya. Lapis atas adalah perencana: ia mempartisi area dan memberi tiap drone satu lajur. \end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Low level: one tilt limit, one structure, two controllers}
  \mins{8}%
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.54\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}\bulletgap\small
        \item Only tilted thrust moves a quadrotor sideways, so capping tilt at
              15\textdegree{} caps acceleration at
              \num{\PlantAmax}\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}
        \item \lqr{} and \hinf{} are designed on the same model and run as the
              same PID
        \item They differ only in their objective --- the model, or the worst
              disturbance
      \end{itemize}
      \vskip0.6ex
      \begin{callout}{Passed upward: four numbers, from the gains}
        {\scriptsize
        $k_v$ \num{\PlantKv}\,s$^{-1}$ \textperiodcentered{}
        $a_{\max}$ \num{\PlantAmax}\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}\\
        $T_{\mathrm{lead}}$ \num{\PlantTlead}\,s \textperiodcentered{}
        $v_c$ \num{\PlantVc}\,m/s}
      \end{callout}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}
      \centering\vskip2mm
      \scalebox{0.66}{\diagfbd}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \narasi{At the low level, a quadrotor can only move sideways by tilting. We limit the tilt to 15 degrees, so the maximum horizontal acceleration is \PlantAmax\space metres per second squared. We design two controllers on the same linear model. LQR minimises a quadratic cost, and H-infinity minimises the effect of the worst-case disturbance. Both are then turned into a standard PID with the same structure, so only the gains are different.}
    {Di lapis bawah, quadrotor hanya bisa bergerak ke samping dengan cara miring. Kami membatasi kemiringan 15 derajat, sehingga percepatan horizontal maksimumnya \PlantAmax\space meter per detik kuadrat. Kami merancang dua pengendali pada model linier yang sama. LQR meminimalkan biaya kuadratik, dan H-infinity meminimalkan efek gangguan terburuk. Keduanya lalu diubah menjadi PID standar dengan struktur yang sama, jadi yang berbeda hanya gain-nya.}
    {Point at the tilted thrust in the figure on the first sentence.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Mid level: a speed limit the drone can actually obey}
  \mins{9}%
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}\bulletgap\footnotesize
        \item Tilt limit + 0.4\,s reaction delay: the fastest approach that
              can still stop, at every distance
        \item Each obstacle, wall or neighbour limits only the
              \emph{approach} speed --- sideways stays free
        \item A small QP picks the safe velocity closest to the plan, 20 times
              per second
      \end{itemize}
      \vskip0.4ex
      \begin{callout}{Why not a simple linear rule}
        It allows approach where the drone can no longer stop (pink band).
        Ours has no free parameter.
      \end{callout}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.50\textwidth}
      \setlength{\figmaxh}{44mm}\figcard{\panelcbfa{width=\linewidth}}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \narasi{Next, the safety layer. The drone has a limited braking acceleration and a reaction delay of about 0.4 seconds. From these, we compute a barrier function: for each distance, the maximum approach speed that still lets the drone stop in time. That's the blue curve. Each obstacle, wall or neighbour becomes one linear constraint on the approach speed, and a small QP finds the safe velocity closest to the planned one. A linear barrier, the pink area, would allow approach where the drone can no longer stop. Ours doesn't, and it has no parameter to tune.}
    {Berikutnya, lapis keselamatan. Drone punya percepatan pengereman yang terbatas dan jeda reaksi sekitar 0,4 detik. Dari keduanya, kami menghitung barrier function: untuk setiap jarak, kecepatan mendekat maksimum yang masih memungkinkan drone berhenti tepat waktu. Itulah kurva biru. Setiap rintangan, dinding, atau tetangga menjadi satu kendala linier pada kecepatan mendekat, dan QP kecil mencari kecepatan aman yang paling dekat dengan kecepatan yang direncanakan. Barrier linier, area merah muda, masih mengizinkan drone mendekat padahal sudah tidak bisa berhenti. Barrier kami tidak, dan tidak punya parameter yang perlu disetel.}
    {Point at the curve, then at the pink band on the last sentence.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{High level: cells, lanes, and re-planning when an agent is lost}
  \mins{8}%
  \centering\setlength{\figmaxh}{40mm}
  \figcard{\includegraphics[width=0.90\textwidth]{concept_high}}
  \figcaption{(a) 7 agents, 54 lanes \textperiodcentered{} (b) agent 4 lost at
    41\,s \textperiodcentered{} (c) agent 5 lost at 58\,s}
  \narasi{The high level plans the coverage. It partitions the area into Voronoi cells, one per drone, and covers each cell with back-and-forth lanes, called boustrophedon lanes. The lanes go around known obstacles. When a drone is lost, its cell is merged with the neighbouring cells, the remaining lanes are re-planned, and nearby drones take them over. The figure shows the plan at the start, and after each of the two losses.}
    {Lapis atas merencanakan cakupan. Ia mempartisi area menjadi sel Voronoi, satu per drone, dan menyapu tiap sel dengan lajur bolak-balik yang disebut lajur boustrophedon. Lajurnya memutari rintangan yang sudah diketahui. Saat sebuah drone hilang, selnya digabung dengan sel tetangga, lajur yang tersisa direncanakan ulang, lalu drone terdekat mengambil alih. Gambar ini menunjukkan rencana di awal, dan setelah masing-masing dari dua kehilangan.}
    {Walk panels a, b, c left to right.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{\hinf{} holds the lane tighter, at a torque cost}
  \mins{9}%
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.44\textwidth}
      \setlength{\figmaxh}{40mm}\figcard{\paneltrackb{width=\linewidth}}
      \figcaption{Lateral RMS against torque effort \textperiodcentered{} marker
        shape is the region}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}\bulletgap\small
        \item Lateral error \LatRedMin--\LatRedMax\% lower with \hinf
        \item Mostly a removed steady offset --- its integral action is
              \KiRatio$\times$ stronger
        \item Torque effort \EffRatioMin--\EffRatioMax$\times$ higher; motor
              saturation below 0.2\%
      \end{itemize}
      \vskip0.6ex
      \begin{callout}{Neither design wins outright}
        \hinf{} is tighter on the lane; \lqr{} is gentler on the airframe.
      \end{callout}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \narasi{So why is H-infinity closer to its lanes? Its lateral error is \LatRedMin\space to \LatRedMax\space percent smaller. But this is mostly not better disturbance rejection. It mostly removes a steady-state offset, because its integral gain is about \KiRatio\space times larger. The cost is \EffRatioMin\space to \EffRatioMax\space times more control effort. So there's no clear winner: H-infinity is more precise, and LQR uses less effort.}
    {Jadi kenapa H-infinity lebih dekat ke lajurnya? Galat lateralnya \LatRedMin\space sampai \LatRedMax\space persen lebih kecil. Tapi sebagian besar bukan karena penolakan gangguan yang lebih baik. Sebagian besar karena ia menghilangkan offset steady-state, sebab gain integralnya sekitar \KiRatio\space kali lebih besar. Harganya, control effort-nya \EffRatioMin\space sampai \EffRatioMax\space kali lebih besar. Jadi tidak ada pemenang yang jelas: H-infinity lebih presisi, dan LQR memakai effort lebih kecil.}
    {Point at the gap between the blue and orange clusters.}
\end{frame}
