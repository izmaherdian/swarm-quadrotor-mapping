\begin{adjustwidth}{0.2in}{0.2in}
{\fontsize{9}{11}\selectfont\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}
Surveying a non-convex site with a quadrotor swarm must remain safe and complete when the
wind is strong, the obstacles are unknown in advance, and agents fail in flight. We present a
three-layer architecture for this task. The high level partitions the region by
polygon-clipped centroidal Voronoi tessellation, plans a boundary-following boustrophedon sweep
in every cell, and reallocates the unswept lanes of failed agents to their neighbours. The mid
level builds an obstacle map from on-board LiDAR and filters every velocity command through a
control barrier function quadratic program whose class-$\mathcal{K}$ function follows from the
tilt limit and dead time of the vehicle. The low level is a cascaded PID whose gains are
synthesised from either the LQR or the $H_\infty$ Riccati equation. Seven quadrotors were flown
in ROS~2/Gazebo over one convex and three non-convex regions under combined hazards:
Gauss--Markov turbulence ($\sigma=2.5$~m/s) over a \WindMeanMag~m/s mean wind, nine unmapped static
obstacles, two moving obstacles, and the loss of two agents during the mission. Seven of the
eight missions finished, with \CovMin--\CovMax\% final coverage after the loss of 28.6\% of the
fleet. \Hinf{} completed all four regions, lowered the RMS lateral sweep error by
\LatRedMin--\LatRedMax\% and finished \TimeRedMin--\TimeRedMax\% sooner than \LQR{}, at
\EffRatioMin--\EffRatioMax\ times its torque effort. One \LQR{} mission was lost when an agent
touched an obstacle, and both controllers grazed the same obstacle in one region; the largest
part of the tracking difference is the rejection of the mean wind. Each configuration was flown
once, so these are single-run observations.
\par}

\headingone{Introduction}
% =====================================================================
Multi-rotor swarms shorten aerial surveys by flying several sensors in parallel, but real
survey sites are rarely rectangles. L-, U- and cross-shaped campuses, plants and quarries
contain re-entrant corners and narrow arms, the wind near the ground is gusty, obstacles are
seldom known before take-off, and vehicles fail. Each of these hazards has been addressed in
isolation. Centroidal Voronoi tessellation (CVT) with Lloyd's iteration distributes agents over
a region \cite{lloydLeastSquaresQuantization1982,duCentroidalVoronoiTessellations1999,cortesCoverageControl2004}
and has been extended to non-convex domains
\cite{breitenmoserVoronoiCoverage2010,caoDistributedWeightedCoverage2023}, including agent
dropout and reinsertion \cite{leeAdaptiveCentroidalVoronoi2024}. Boustrophedon sweeps are the
standard way to cover a cell
\cite{chosetBoustrophedonDecomposition1998,chosetCoverageSurvey2001,galceranCoveragePathPlanning2013,huRoutePlanning2021},
also for aerial visual coverage \cite{papatheodorouCooperativeVisual2017}. Control barrier
functions (CBF) enforce safety as a quadratic program (QP) on the control input
\cite{amesControlBarrierFunction2017,amesControlBarrierFunctions2019}, with reciprocal
multi-robot forms \cite{wangSafetyBarrierCertificates2017,zhouBufferedVoronoiCells2017}.
Linear-quadratic and $H_\infty$ synthesis are classical routes to quadrotor attitude and
position control \cite{belkheiriLinearizationControl2012,doyleStateSpaceHinf1989}, and fault
tolerance in swarms is an active topic \cite{okeeffeAnticipatingDegradation2025}.

What is less reported is how these pieces behave \emph{together} on a physically simulated
vehicle, where a velocity command cannot be realised instantly, a barrier that assumes it can
is violated, and a planner that assumes perfect tracking loses coverage to wind drift. This
paper contributes (i) an integrated architecture that runs polygon-aware CVT partitioning,
boundary-following boustrophedon sweeps and lane-level fault reallocation above a LiDAR-mapped
CBF-QP safety filter whose class-$\mathcal{K}$ function is derived from the identified
closed-loop dynamics of the vehicle; (ii) a side-by-side comparison of PID gains synthesised
from the LQR and the $H_\infty$ Riccati equations, both on the linear model and inside the full
swarm; and (iii) a data-traceable evaluation of eight full missions under combined turbulence,
unmapped obstacles, moving obstacles and agent loss, including an analysis of the failures that
occurred. Every number reported is computed by a published script from the recorded telemetry.

\headingtwo{System Architecture}
Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the three layers. They are defined per agent: each agent owns a
cell, a lane queue, a safety filter and a flight controller, and the filter of agent $i$ only
uses neighbours within 6~m and obstacles within 10~m. In the simulation reported here the high
and mid levels of all agents are \emph{hosted} in one ROS~2 node \cite{macenskiROS2_2022}
running at 20~Hz, which shares the coverage grid, the obstacle map and failure notices without
communication delay; the low level runs as one node per vehicle at 250~Hz on the odometry
stream. A distributed deployment of the upper layers is future work.

\headingtwo{High Level: Partition, Sweep and Fault-Tolerant Cooperative Coverage}
Given the region polygon $\mathcal{Q}$ and generators $\mathbf{g}_i$, the cell of agent $i$ is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{P}_i=\bigl\{\mathbf{q}\in\mathcal{Q}:\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{g}_i\|\le\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{g}_j\|,\ \forall j\ne i\bigr\},
    \label{eq:vor}
\end{equation}
computed by clipping the bisector cells of the bounding box to $\mathcal{Q}$. Lloyd's iteration
$\mathbf{g}_i\leftarrow\mathrm{centroid}(\mathcal{P}_i)$ (25 iterations from deterministic
k-means++ seeds) minimises the coverage cost
$\sum_i\int_{\mathcal{P}_i}\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{g}_i\|^2\,d\mathbf{q}$
\cite{cortesCoverageControl2004}, and agents are assigned to cells by the Hungarian algorithm on
their post-take-off positions. Each cell is shrunk by 0.35~m and swept by east--west lanes at
most 1.45~m apart. Because the first and last lanes follow the cell's own bottom and top
boundary chains rather than a horizontal line, the triangular wedges that a fixed-pitch sweep
leaves at slanted Voronoi edges are also flown. With a sensing radius of $r_s=0.95$~m the
swath is 1.90~m, giving at least 24\% lateral overlap. Coverage is scored on a 0.30~m grid: a
cell inside $\mathcal{Q}$ counts once its centre has been within $r_s$ of a live, sweeping agent
flying above 0.8~m; cells under static obstacles are excluded from the denominator.

When agent $k$ fails, Fault-Tolerant Cooperative Coverage (FT-CC) (1) keeps the unfinished
recovery lanes that $k$ was carrying, (2) erases the coverage recorded inside $\mathcal{P}_k$,
treating the data of the lost vehicle as lost, (3) merges all failed cells into
$\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{rec}}=\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{P}_k$, (4) plans boustrophedon lanes over
$\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{rec}}$ and discards lanes already at least 80\% covered, and (5) selects
helpers among the neighbours of the failed cells, ranked by distance to the recovery centroid:
one helper for a single loss with at most five lanes, otherwise two, and three after a second
loss. The lanes are split into contiguous serpentine blocks, one per helper; a helper that has
finished its own cell flies to its block directly, otherwise the block is appended to its
queue.

\headingtwo{Mid Level: Obstacle Mapping and CBF-QP Safety Filter}
\headingthree{Obstacle Map}
Each 360-ray scan is cleaned of returns within 0.55~m of other agents and 0.80~m of the arena
boundary, split into clusters, and each cluster is fitted with a circle. Detections are
associated into one swarm-wide track list; a track is confirmed after 12 hits and rejected if it
moves. The static obstacle rows of the QP use only confirmed tracks, so the swarm starts with an
empty map. The planner deliberately ignores obstacles: lanes are laid over the whole cell and
avoidance is left entirely to the safety filter. The two moving obstacles are supplied to the QP from simulator odometry, i.e.\ they
are assumed to be tracked perfectly.

\headingthree{Actuator-Consistent Barrier}
With the reference rule $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{ref}}=\mathbf{p}+T_{\mathrm{lead}}\mathbf{u}$, the
outer PID makes the commanded-velocity response first order with an acceleration limit,
$\dot v=\mathrm{sat}_{\pm a_{\max}}\bigl(k_v(u-v)\bigr)$, where
\begin{equation}
    k_v=gK_d,\qquad a_{\max}=g\tan\theta_{\max},\qquad
    T_{\mathrm{lead}}=\frac{K_d-k_{\mathrm{ff}}}{K_p},\qquad v_c=\frac{a_{\max}}{k_v},
    \label{eq:plant}
\end{equation}
with $\theta_{\max}=15^\circ$ and feed-forward gain $k_{\mathrm{ff}}=0.15$~rad\,s/m. Evaluated
with the LQR outer-loop gains (for both controllers) this gives $k_v=\PlantKv$~s$^{-1}$, $a_{\max}=\PlantAmax$~m/s$^2$,
$T_{\mathrm{lead}}=\PlantTlead$~s and $v_c=\PlantVc$~m/s. The stopping distance from speed $s>v_c$
is $D(s)=(s^2+v_c^2)/2a$. Requiring that the distance covered during the dead time $T_d$ plus
$D(s)$ fit inside the clearance $h$ yields the admissible approach speed
\begin{equation}
    \varphi(h)=
    \begin{cases}
        \max\Bigl(0,\,-aT_d+\sqrt{\max\bigl(0,\,a^2T_d^2+2ah-v_c^2\bigr)}\Bigr), & h\ge 0,\\
        \gamma_r\,h, & h<0,
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
with a lumped dead time $T_d=0.40$~s for sampling, filtering and the attitude loop. It is
zero below a finite clearance, a property no linear $\alpha(h)=\gamma h$ has; the
branch $h<0$ ($\gamma_r=3.5$~s$^{-1}$) demands an outward velocity if the set has been left.
The budget $a$ is class dependent,
$a=\max\bigl(0.15a_{\max},\,\mu a_{\max}-|a_{\mathrm{obs}}|-|a_{\mathrm{wind}}|\bigr)$ with
$\mu=0.75$ (0.85 for walls) and a wind allowance of 0.5~m/s$^2$.

\headingthree{Quadratic Program}
For an obstacle or neighbour at $\mathbf{o}$ with unit normal
$\hat{\mathbf{n}}=(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{o})/\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{o}\|$, the condition
$\dot h\ge-\varphi(h)$ is the linear row
\begin{equation}
    -\hat{\mathbf{n}}^{\top}\mathbf{u}\le\lambda\,\varphi(h)-\hat{\mathbf{n}}^{\top}\mathbf{v}_{o},
    \qquad h=\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{o}\|-(\rho+r_d+\delta),
    \label{eq:row}
\end{equation}
with drone radius $r_d=0.22$~m, obstacle radius $\rho$ (fitted radius $+0.05$~m for mapped
cylinders, 0.50~m for the moving ones), margins $\delta=0.35$~m (static) and 0.75~m (moving),
and $\lambda=1$ for obstacles. Neighbour rows are reciprocal ($\lambda_{ij}+\lambda_{ji}=1$,
weighted by mission state) and are issued at a hard radius of 0.70~m and a soft radius of
1.20~m. Arena walls and the end of the current lane give half-plane rows
$\hat{\mathbf{a}}^\top\mathbf{u}\le\varphi(h)$, and inscribed polygons bound speed (3~m/s) and
the per-tick change of $\mathbf{u}$. Each agent then solves
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{u}^\star=\arg\min_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^2}\bigl\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{z}\bigr\|^2
    \ \ \text{s.t.}\ \ A\mathbf{u}\le\mathbf{b},\qquad
    \mathbf{z}=\frac{\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{des}}+\mathbf{t}+w\,\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{prev}}}{1+w},
    \label{eq:qp}
\end{equation}
exactly, by enumerating the unconstrained point, the projections onto every row and the
pairwise vertices. The tangential bias $\mathbf{t}$ (applied within a 60$^\circ$ cone of a
head-on approach) breaks symmetric deadlocks and only enters the objective, never $A$ or
$\mathbf{b}$; $w=0.25$ smooths successive commands. If the QP is infeasible, soft rows are
released (tier~1); if still infeasible, the violation of the hard rows is minimised (tier~2),
and a scaled previous command is the last resort (tier~3).

\headingtwo{Low Level: LQR and $H_\infty$ Gain Synthesis}
Each axis is modelled about hover as a double integrator $\dot{\mathbf{x}}=A\mathbf{x}+Bu$
(e.g.\ $B=[0,\ g]^\top$ from pitch to $x$, $B=[0,\ 1/I_y]^\top$ from torque to pitch) and
augmented with an integrator on the input, $\mathbf{x}_a=[\mathbf{x}^\top,u]^\top$. The LQR
design solves the algebraic Riccati equation (ARE) for $K_a=R^{-1}B_a^\top P$. The $H_\infty$
design treats a disturbance $w$ entering through the input channel, $B_w=[B^\top,0]^\top$, and
solves
\begin{equation}
    A_a^\top P+PA_a+Q_a-P\bigl(B_aR^{-1}B_a^\top-\gamma^{-2}B_wB_w^\top\bigr)P=0,
    \label{eq:hare}
\end{equation}
rejecting solutions for which $A_a-B_aK_a$ is not Hurwitz \cite{doyleStateSpaceHinf1989}.
The attenuation level is set to $\gamma=1.3\,\gamma_{\min}$, where $\gamma_{\min}$ is found by
bisection. The state-feedback gain is mapped to PID form through the pseudo-inverse of the
output-derivative matrix
$\Gamma=[C^\top,(CA)^\top,(CA^2)^\top;\,0,(CB)^\top,(CAB)^\top]$. The outer loop maps position
error to a tilt reference with derivative action on measured velocity, clipped to 15$^\circ$;
the inner loop is PD on attitude (the synthesised inner integral gain is not used); altitude and
yaw loops are designed the same way. A mixer that preserves roll/pitch authority before
collective thrust and yaw converts the wrench into rotor speeds. Weights, attenuation levels and
the resulting gains are listed in Table~\ref{tab:gains}.

\headingtwo{Simulation Setup}
% =====================================================================
Seven 3DR Iris quadrotors ($m=1.50$~kg, $I_{xx}=I_{yy}=0.0291$, $I_{zz}=0.0552$~kg\,m$^2$, arm
0.22~m, $k_f=8.549\times10^{-6}$~N\,s$^2$, rotor speed $\le1100$~rad/s) are simulated in Gazebo
\cite{koenigGazebo2004} with ROS~2. Controllers read ground-truth odometry; no state estimator
is in the loop. Missions start from a launch pad south of the region, cruise at 2.0~m and sweep
at 1.6~m/s. Four regions inside a $28\times28$~m box are used (Fig.~\ref{fig:traj}): \emph{rect}
(784~m$^2$), \emph{L-shape} (588~m$^2$), \emph{U-shape} (624~m$^2$, 10~m wide notch) and
\emph{plus} (460~m$^2$, 10~m wide arms), each with nine vertical cylinders ($r=0.40$~m, 4~m
tall) placed inside the region. All missions use the same combined-hazard scheme:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=1pt,parsep=0pt]
\item \emph{Wind.} Each horizontal axis follows a first-order Gauss--Markov process
      ($\sigma=2.5$~m/s, correlation time 0.5~s, seed 42), a Dryden-like surrogate
      \cite{milhdbk1797,beardSmallUnmannedAircraft2012}, plus a constant $-2$~m/s mean on both
      axes from $t=5$~s; the vertical component is scaled by 0.4. The wind is spatially uniform
      and applied through the Gazebo wind-effects plugin. The recorded wind had mean
      $(\WindMeanX,\WindMeanY)$~m/s and standard deviation $(\WindStdX,\WindStdY)$~m/s, with
      horizontal peaks of \WindMaxH~m/s.
\item \emph{Moving obstacles.} Two kinematic cylinders ($r=0.45$~m) oscillate along the region
      diagonals with amplitude 10~m (12~m, offset by 1~m, in \emph{plus}) at 0.15 and 0.11~rad/s,
      i.e.\ peak speeds of 1.6--2.6~m/s.
\item \emph{Agent loss.} When coverage first reaches 18\% and 32\%, one pre-selected agent is
      killed (rotor speeds set to zero, fail-stop) and the coordinator is notified at once.
      Victims were chosen to own comparable cells next to the launch pad (Table~\ref{tab:results}).
\end{itemize}
Both controllers use the same planner, filter and plant model; Eq.~\eqref{eq:plant} is evaluated
with the LQR outer-loop gains in both cases. Each region--controller pair was flown once
($N=1$) and recorded on video. The two \emph{plus} missions also include three transit guards
added before they were flown (a stall detector, a speed reduction above 16$^\circ$ tilt and a
20~s start-wait timeout). Metrics are computed from 20~Hz controller logs, discarding the rows
after an agent is killed and the first 20~s. Lateral error is the deviation from the lane
reference during steady east--west sweeps (heading stable for 1.5~s, speed above 0.6~m/s);
torque effort is the time average of $\tau_x^2+\tau_y^2$; obstacle distance is measured from the
drone centre to the cylinder surface. The collision geometry of the airframe reaches
\BodyHalf~m (body) to \RotorReach~m (rotor tips) from its centre, so a surface distance below
these values means certain or possible contact.

\headingtwo{Mission Completion and Fault Recovery}
Table~\ref{tab:results} and Fig.~\ref{fig:traj} summarise the eight missions. Seven finished
with every surviving agent back at its cell centroid and final coverage between \CovMin\% and
\CovMax\%, although two of seven agents (28.6\% of the fleet) were lost at 18\% and 32\%
coverage. In every mission the obstacle map contained all nine cylinders, with centre and radius
errors within the 0.1~m resolution of the log, plus at most two confirmed tracks that matched no
cylinder; no completed mission needed a tier-3 QP fallback. Figure~\ref{fig:coverage} shows the recovery mechanism: each kill causes a small
drop, because the coverage inside the failed cell is erased, after which the helpers resume the
original slope; Fig.~\ref{fig:env} shows the resulting coverage maps. The \LQR{} U-shape mission ended just below the 97\% target (\CovUshapeLqr\%)
when all agents had exhausted their lanes; its final coverage map shows thin uncovered strips
along the boundaries between neighbouring cells in the left arm, which are nearly absent in the
\Hinf{} map of the same region. We did not isolate their cause. The \LQR{} rect mission was aborted at
\TmisRectLqr~s and is analysed below.

\headingtwo{Tracking under Turbulence: LQR versus $H_\infty$ Gains}
The two designs differ clearly in how tightly they hold a lane (Fig.~\ref{fig:tracking}a). The
median lateral error was \LatMedHinfMin--\LatMedHinfMax~cm with \Hinf{} and
\LatMedLqrMin--\LatMedLqrMax~cm with \LQR{}, and the RMS error fell from
\LatRmsLqrMin--\LatRmsLqrMax~cm to \LatRmsHinfMin--\LatRmsHinfMax~cm, a
\LatRedMin--\LatRedMax\% reduction that holds in all four regions. Splitting the error into its mean and its fluctuation shows where the
difference lies. \LQR{} flew with a downwind offset of $\LatMeanLqrMax$ to $\LatMeanLqrMin$~cm
in every region (the mean wind blows towards $-y$), whereas \Hinf{} stayed within
\LatMeanHinfMin--\LatMeanHinfMax~cm of the lane; the standard deviation differed less
(\LatStdHinfMin--\LatStdHinfMax{} vs \LatStdLqrMin--\LatStdLqrMax~cm). The offset is a
low-frequency effect: the $H_\infty$ outer integral gain is \KiRatio{} times larger, and the
\LQR{} node additionally freezes its position integrator whenever the error exceeds 0.25~m,
which was the case in \IntegOffLqrMin--\IntegOffLqrMax\% of its sweep samples. The mean sweep speed was
\VHinfMin--\VHinfMax~m/s against \VLqrMin--\VLqrMax~m/s for a 1.6~m/s command, which accounts
for the \TimeRedMin--\TimeRedMax\% shorter missions where both finished. Altitude error and the
95th-percentile tilt were essentially equal, since both are bounded by the same tilt limit. The linear analysis in Table~\ref{tab:gains} explains the direction of the result:
the $H_\infty$ gains lower the worst-case gain from disturbance acceleration to position by a
factor of \HnormRatio{} and the steady-state position deviation under the Gauss--Markov input by
\SigxRatio{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:tracking}c).

The price is visible in the same table. \Hinf{} used \EffRatioMin--\EffRatioMax{} times the
torque effort of \LQR{} in the completed pairs (Fig.~\ref{fig:tracking}b), although motors were
saturated in less than 0.2\% of samples in every mission, and its outer loop has a higher
crossover (\WcHinf{} vs \WcLqr~rad/s) with smaller phase and gain margins. The measured error
reduction is also far smaller than the linear factor, because the tilt limit, the reference
pre-filter, the rate limits of the QP and the wind force model are not in the linear model. Two
further caveats limit the attribution: the synthesis weights $Q$ and $R$ are not identical in
the two designs, and the integrator gating described above belongs to the \LQR{} node, not to
the synthesis. The comparison is therefore between two complete gain designs; this study does not
isolate the contribution of the attenuation level $\gamma$ from that of higher loop gain.

\headingtwo{Safety: Clearance, Contact and Upset Events}
No two agents came closer than \VtwovLshapeLqr~m (\LQR{}, L-shape) and all minima stayed above
the 0.70~m hard radius, so there was no inter-agent contact. Against static cylinders the
minimum surface distance under \Hinf{} was \ObssurfRectHinf, \ObssurfLshapeHinf{} and
\ObssurfUshapeHinf~m in rect, L-shape and U-shape, clear of the airframe, while under \LQR{} it
was \ObssurfRectLqr, \ObssurfLshapeLqr{} and \ObssurfUshapeLqr~m. In \emph{plus} both
controllers passed within \ObssurfPlusHinf~m of the central cylinder, inside the rotor envelope.
Figure~\ref{fig:events} shows the upsets that followed, and the coordinator log shows a common
pattern: each contact happened while the agent was stepping from one lane to the next beside a
cylinder, and in each case the QP had run in tier~2 for \TierSpanMin--\TierSpanMax~s with that cylinder
as the binding row. In the \LQR{} rect mission, agent 2 turned into its third lane next to the cylinder at
$(0,2.5)$, reached a surface distance of \UpRectLqrSurf~m, i.e.\ body contact, tilted to
\UpRectLqrTilt$^\circ$ and fell; the crash detector aborted the mission. In \emph{plus}, agent 2
of both missions stepped from its fourth to its fifth lane next to the central cylinder: under
\LQR{} the tilt reached \UpPlusLqrTilt$^\circ$ with little height loss, under \Hinf{} it
reached \UpPlusHinfTilt$^\circ$, the vehicle dropped to \UpPlusHinfZmin~m and regained cruise
altitude within about 3~s. With one run per case, none of these events can be attributed to
the controller. They are better explained by the design choice that lanes are planned without
obstacle knowledge, which lets lane ends and steps fall inside the safety margin of a
cylinder, combined with a barrier built on a 0.22~m disc and a fixed 0.5~m/s$^2$ wind allowance
that gusts of up to \WindMaxH~m/s exceed. A further \UpRectLqrSecondTilt$^\circ$ upset
(\LQR{}, rect, agent 6 at \UpRectLqrSecondT~s) occurred in transit away from any cylinder and
was recovered. Moving obstacles also entered their 0.75~m margin: the QP logged negative barrier
values against them in \NRunsHdynNeg{} of the eight missions, the lowest ($\HdynMinLogged$~m)
corresponding to a centre-to-surface distance of \DynSurfMinLogged~m, at the rotor envelope.
Because these values are only logged while the QP runs in tier~2, they bound the true minimum
from above. A collision envelope matching the rotor tips, obstacle-aware lane ends and a
gust-dependent acceleration budget are the obvious remedies.

\headingtwo{Limitations}
The results rest on one run per configuration with a fixed wind seed, perfect state feedback,
perfectly tracked moving obstacles, instantaneous failure detection, a geometric coverage model
without camera or photogrammetric error, and centralised execution of the upper layers. The planner and barrier components were not
ablated, and no coverage method from the literature was re-implemented for comparison.
Statistical claims, estimator and communication effects, and a distributed implementation remain
to be addressed.

% =====================================================================
\headingone{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================
A three-layer architecture combining polygon-clipped Voronoi partitioning, boundary-following
sweeps, lane-level fault reallocation, a LiDAR-mapped actuator-consistent CBF-QP and
Riccati-synthesised PID control was evaluated in eight simulated missions with seven
quadrotors under strong turbulence, unmapped and moving obstacles, and the loss of two agents.
Seven missions finished with \CovMin--\CovMax\% coverage. Gains synthesised from the $H_\infty$
Riccati equation held the lanes \LatRedMin--\LatRedMax\% more tightly and shortened missions by
\TimeRedMin--\TimeRedMax\% compared with LQR gains, at several times the torque effort and with
smaller stability margins; both designs suffered obstacle contacts in gusts, one of which ended
an LQR mission. Future work will repeat the campaign over many wind seeds, enlarge the collision
envelope and add wind to the barrier model, distribute the upper layers onto the vehicles, and
move to flight tests with on-board state estimation.
