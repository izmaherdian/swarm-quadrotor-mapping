\begin{adjustwidth}{0.2in}{0.2in}
{\fontsize{9}{11}\selectfont\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}
Surveying a non-convex site with a quadrotor swarm must remain safe and complete when the
wind is strong, obstacles are unknown in advance, and agents fail in flight. We present a
three-layer architecture for this task. The high level partitions the region by polygon-clipped
centroidal Voronoi tessellation, plans boundary-following boustrophedon sweeps and reallocates
the unswept lanes of failed agents. The mid level maps obstacles with on-board LiDAR and filters
every velocity command through a control barrier function quadratic program whose
admissible approach-speed function follows from the tilt limit and dead time of the vehicle. The low
level is a cascaded PID with gains synthesised from the LQR or the $H_\infty$ Riccati equation.
Seven quadrotors were flown in ROS~2/Gazebo over one convex and three non-convex regions under
Gauss--Markov turbulence ($\sigma=2.5$~m/s) with a recorded mean wind of \WindMeanMag~m/s, nine unmapped
static and two moving obstacles, and two scheduled agent losses. Seven of eight missions
finished with \CovMin--\CovMax\% coverage despite losing 28.6\% of the fleet. \Hinf{} completed
all four regions; in the three regions where both controllers finished, it lowered the RMS lateral
sweep error by \LatRedDoneMin--\LatRedDoneMax\%, mostly by rejecting the mean wind, and finished
\TimeRedMin--\TimeRedMax\% sooner, at \EffRatioMin--\EffRatioMax\ times the torque effort of \LQR{}.
One \LQR{} mission was stopped after an agent struck an obstacle, and in three other missions an
agent passed within rotor reach of a cylinder. Each configuration was flown once, so these are
single-run observations. The source code is available at
\ifanon [link removed for double-blind review]\else\url{https://github.com/izmaherdian/swarm-quadrotor-mapping}\fi.
\par}
\end{adjustwidth}

\headingone{Introduction}
% =====================================================================
Multi-rotor swarms shorten aerial surveys, but real sites are rarely rectangles: they have
re-entrant corners, gusty wind, obstacles unknown before take-off, and vehicles that fail.
Centroidal Voronoi tessellation (CVT) with Lloyd's iteration \cite{lloydLeastSquaresQuantization1982}
distributes agents over a region and has been combined with obstacles and actuator faults
\cite{baiAdaptiveCoverageObstacle2022}, safety constraints \cite{baiSafeAdaptiveCoverage2023},
non-convex domains \cite{caoDistributedWeightedCoverage2023} and agent dropout
\cite{leeAdaptiveCentroidalVoronoi2024}; boustrophedon sweeps remain a standard way to cover a cell
\cite{tanCoveragePathPlanningReview2021,huRoutePlanning2021}, including cooperative UAV surveys
\cite{fevgasCoveragePathPlanningUAV2022} with re-planning after failures
\cite{lunaMultiUAVReplanning2024}. Control barrier functions (CBF) enforce safety as a quadratic
program (QP) on the input \cite{amesControlBarrierFunction2017,hsuSafetyFilter2024}, with
high-order \cite{xiaoHighOrderCBF2022}, multi-robot \cite{gargLearningSafeMultiRobot2024} and
quadrotor forms for moving obstacles \cite{tayalCollisionConeUAV2024} and wind gusts
\cite{abichandaniMultiQuadrotorWind2023}; LQR and $H_\infty$ synthesis \cite{doyleStateSpaceHinf1989} remain common for multirotor
control under wind \cite{zyadatQuadplaneWind2023}, and swarm fault
tolerance is an active topic \cite{okeeffeAnticipatingDegradation2025}. Hierarchical quadcopter
swarms with a reactive potential-field planner above cascaded PID loops fly formations through
clutter and gusts, yet purely reactive planners remain prone to U-shaped traps
\cite{herdianFormationIAPFERC2026}.

What is less reported is how these pieces behave \emph{together} on a physically simulated
vehicle, where velocity commands are not realised instantly and wind drift costs coverage. This
paper contributes (i) an architecture running polygon-aware CVT partitioning, boundary-following
sweeps and lane-level fault reallocation above a LiDAR-mapped CBF-QP whose admissible approach
speed is derived from the closed-loop model of the vehicle; (ii) a comparison of PID gains
from the LQR and $H_\infty$ Riccati equations, on the linear model and inside the swarm; and
(iii) an evaluation of eight missions under combined hazards, including their failures.

Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the three layers, defined per agent: a cell, a lane queue, a safety
filter using neighbours within 6~m and obstacles within 10~m, and a flight controller. The upper
layers of all agents run in one 20~Hz ROS~2 node \cite{macenskiROS2_2022}, sharing the coverage

\headingtwo{High Level: Partition, Sweep and Fault-Tolerant Cooperative Coverage}
Given the region polygon $\mathcal{Q}$ and generators $\mathbf{g}_i$, the cell of agent $i$ is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{P}_i=\bigl\{\mathbf{q}\in\mathcal{Q}:\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{g}_i\|\le\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{g}_j\|,\ \forall j\ne i\bigr\},
    \label{eq:vor}
\end{equation}
computed by clipping bisector half-planes and intersecting the result with $\mathcal{Q}$. Lloyd's
iteration $\mathbf{g}_i\leftarrow\mathrm{centroid}(\mathcal{P}_i)$ (25 iterations from
deterministic k-means++ seeds) minimises
$\sum_i\int_{\mathcal{P}_i}\|\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{g}_i\|^2\,d\mathbf{q}$
\cite{lloydLeastSquaresQuantization1982}, and agents are assigned to cells by the Hungarian algorithm.
Each cell is shrunk by 0.35~m and swept by east--west lanes at most 1.45~m apart; its first and
last lanes follow the cell boundary, so the wedges that a fixed-pitch sweep leaves at slanted
Voronoi edges are flown too (Fig.~\ref{fig:concept_high}a). With a sensing radius $r_s=0.95$~m
the swath is 1.90~m (overlap of at least 0.45~m). Coverage is scored on a 0.30~m grid: a grid cell
inside $\mathcal{Q}$ counts once its centre has been within $r_s$ of a live, sweeping agent
flying above 0.8~m; grid cells under static obstacles are excluded.

When agent $k$ fails, Fault-Tolerant Cooperative Coverage (FT-CC) keeps the recovery lanes that
$k$ still carried, erases the coverage recorded inside $\mathcal{P}_k$ (the data of the lost
vehicle are treated as lost), merges all failed cells, re-plans lanes over their union and drops
lanes that are already at least 80\% covered. The helpers are the neighbours of the failed cells
closest to the recovery centroid (one for a single loss with at most five lanes, otherwise two,
and three after a second loss). They receive contiguous serpentine blocks, which they fly at
once if their own cell is finished and append to their queue otherwise
(Fig.~\ref{fig:concept_high}b,c).

\headingtwo{Mid Level: Obstacle Mapping and CBF-QP Safety Filter}
\emph{Obstacle map.} Each 360-ray scan is cleaned of returns within 0.55~m of other agents and
0.80~m of the arena boundary, clustered, and fitted with circles; detections form one swarm-wide
track list, a track is confirmed after 12 hits and rejected if it moves, and only confirmed tracks
enter the QP, so the swarm starts with an empty map. The planner deliberately ignores obstacles,
leaving avoidance to the safety filter. The two moving obstacles are taken from simulator
odometry, i.e.\ assumed perfectly tracked.

\emph{Actuator-consistent barrier.} With the reference rule $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{ref}}=\mathbf{p}+T_{\mathrm{lead}}\mathbf{u}$, the
outer PID makes the commanded-velocity response first order with an acceleration limit,
$\dot v=\mathrm{sat}_{\pm a_{\max}}\bigl(k_v(u-v)\bigr)$, where
\begin{equation}
    k_v=gK_d,\qquad a_{\max}=g\tan\theta_{\max},\qquad
    T_{\mathrm{lead}}=\frac{K_d-k_{\mathrm{ff}}}{K_p},\qquad v_c=\frac{a_{\max}}{k_v},
    \label{eq:plant}
\end{equation}
with $\theta_{\max}=0.262$~rad ($15^\circ$) and $k_{\mathrm{ff}}=0.15$~rad\,s/m. With the LQR outer-loop gains
(used for both controllers) $k_v=\PlantKv$~s$^{-1}$, $a_{\max}=\PlantAmax$~m/s$^2$,
$T_{\mathrm{lead}}=\PlantTlead$~s and $v_c=\PlantVc$~m/s. The stopping distance from speed
$s>v_c$ is $D(s)=(s^2+v_c^2)/2a$; requiring that the distance travelled during a lumped dead time
$T_d=0.40$~s plus $D(s)$ fit inside the clearance $h$ gives the admissible approach speed
\begin{equation}
    \varphi(h)=
    \begin{cases}
        \max\Bigl(0,\,-aT_d+\sqrt{\max\bigl(0,\,a^2T_d^2+2ah-v_c^2\bigr)}\Bigr), & h\ge 0,\\
        \gamma_r\,h, & h<0,
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
which is exactly zero for $h\le v_c^2/2a$, unlike a linear $\gamma h$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:concept_cbf}a). It is continuous and non-decreasing with $\varphi(0)=0$, but because
of this flat segment it is not strictly increasing and hence not a class-$\mathcal{K}$ function in the
strict sense; the branch $h<0$ ($\gamma_r=3.5$~s$^{-1}$) demands an outward
velocity once the set has been left. The budget is
$a=\max\bigl(0.15a_{\max},\,\mu a_{\max}-|a_{\mathrm{obs}}|-|a_{\mathrm{wind}}|\bigr)$ with
$\mu=0.75$ (0.85 for walls) and a wind allowance of 0.5~m/s$^2$.

\emph{Quadratic program.} For an obstacle or neighbour at $\mathbf{o}$ with
$\hat{\mathbf{n}}=(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{o})/\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{o}\|$, the condition
$\dot h\ge-\varphi(h)$ is the linear row
\begin{equation}
    -\hat{\mathbf{n}}^{\top}\mathbf{u}\le\lambda\,\varphi(h)-\hat{\mathbf{n}}^{\top}\mathbf{v}_{o},
    \qquad h=\|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{o}\|-(\rho+r_d+\delta),
    \label{eq:row}
\end{equation}
with drone radius $r_d=0.22$~m, obstacle radius $\rho$ (fitted radius $+0.05$~m for mapped
cylinders, 0.50~m for moving ones), margins $\delta=0.35$~m (static) and 0.75~m (moving), and
$\lambda=1$ for obstacles. Neighbour rows are reciprocal ($\lambda_{ij}+\lambda_{ji}=1$, weighted
by mission state) at a hard radius of 0.70~m and a soft radius of 1.20~m; walls and the end of
the current lane give half-plane rows, and inscribed polygons bound the speed (3~m/s) and the
per-tick change of $\mathbf{u}$. Each agent then solves
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{u}^\star=\arg\min_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^2}\bigl\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{z}\bigr\|^2
    \ \ \text{s.t.}\ \ A\mathbf{u}\le\mathbf{b},\qquad
    \mathbf{z}=\frac{\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{des}}+\mathbf{t}+w\,\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{prev}}}{1+w},
    \label{eq:qp}
\end{equation}
exactly, by enumerating the unconstrained point, the projections onto every row and the pairwise
vertices (Fig.~\ref{fig:concept_cbf}b). A tangential bias $\mathbf{t}$, applied within a
60$^\circ$ cone of a head-on approach, breaks symmetric deadlocks and enters only the objective;
$w=0.25$ smooths successive commands. If the QP is infeasible, soft rows are released (tier~1),
then the violation of the hard rows is minimised (tier~2), and a scaled previous command is the
last resort (tier~3).

\headingtwo{Low Level: LQR and $H_\infty$ Gain Synthesis}
Each axis is modelled about hover as a double integrator $\dot{\mathbf{x}}=A\mathbf{x}+Bu$
(e.g.\ $B=[0,\ g]^\top$ from pitch to $x$, $B=[0,\ 1/I_y]^\top$ from torque to pitch) and
augmented with an integrator on the input, $\mathbf{x}_a=[\mathbf{x}^\top,u]^\top$. The LQR
design solves the algebraic Riccati equation (ARE) for $K_a=R^{-1}B_a^\top P$. The $H_\infty$
design treats a disturbance entering through the input channel, $B_w=[B^\top,0]^\top$, and
solves
\begin{equation}
    A_a^\top P+PA_a+Q_a-P\bigl(B_aR^{-1}B_a^\top-\gamma^{-2}B_wB_w^\top\bigr)P=0,
    \label{eq:hare}
\end{equation}
rejecting solutions for which $A_a-B_aK_a$ is not Hurwitz \cite{doyleStateSpaceHinf1989}, with
$\gamma=1.3\,\gamma_{\min}$ and $\gamma_{\min}$ found by bisection. The state-feedback gain is
mapped to PID form through the pseudo-inverse of
$\Gamma=[C^\top,(CA)^\top,(CA^2)^\top;\,0,(CB)^\top,(CAB)^\top]$; the synthesised inner integral
gain is not used. Figure~\ref{fig:tick} shows where the gains enter the signal path of one control
step, and Table~\ref{tab:gains} lists weights, attenuation levels and gains.

\headingtwo{Simulation Setup}
% =====================================================================
Seven 3DR Iris quadrotors ($m=1.50$~kg, $I_{xx}=I_{yy}=0.0291$, $I_{zz}=0.0552$~kg\,m$^2$, arm
0.22~m, $k_f=8.549\times10^{-6}$~N\,s$^2$, rotor speed $\le1100$~rad/s) are simulated in Gazebo
\cite{koenigGazebo2004} with ROS~2. Controllers read ground-truth odometry; no state estimator
is in the loop. Missions start from a launch pad south of the region, cruise at 2.0~m and sweep
at 1.6~m/s. Four regions inside a $28\times28$~m box are used (Fig.~\ref{fig:traj}): \emph{rect}
(784~m$^2$), \emph{L-shape} (588~m$^2$), \emph{U-shape} (624~m$^2$, 10~m wide notch) and
\emph{plus} (460~m$^2$, 10~m wide arms), each with nine vertical cylinders ($r=0.40$~m, 4~m
tall) placed inside the region. All missions use the same combined-hazard scheme:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=1pt,parsep=0pt]
\item \emph{Wind.} A first-order Gauss--Markov process per horizontal axis ($\sigma=2.5$~m/s,
      correlation time 0.5~s, seed 42), a surrogate for the Dryden and von K\'arm\'an
      models used in quadrotor studies \cite{mirandaMoyaTurbulentWind2023}, plus a constant $-2$~m/s mean on each
      axis (2.8~m/s horizontally) from $t=5$~s, uniform in space and applied through the Gazebo wind-effects plugin
      (vertical component scaled by 0.4). Recorded mean $(\WindMeanX,\WindMeanY)$~m/s, standard
      deviation $(\WindStdX,\WindStdY)$~m/s, horizontal peaks \WindMaxH~m/s.
\item \emph{Moving obstacles.} Two kinematic cylinders ($r=0.45$~m) oscillate along the region
      diagonals with amplitude 10~m on each axis (12~m, offset by 1~m, in \emph{plus}) at 0.15 and
      0.11~rad/s, i.e.\ peak speeds along the diagonal of 1.6--2.5~m/s.
\item \emph{Agent loss.} When coverage first reaches 18\% and 32\%, one pre-selected agent is
      killed (rotor speeds set to zero, fail-stop) and the coordinator is notified at once.
      Victims were chosen to own comparable cells next to the launch pad (Table~\ref{tab:results});
      in \emph{rect} the first victim differed (4 vs.\ 7), so that pair is not like-for-like.
\end{itemize}
Both controllers share the planner, filter and plant model (Eq.~\eqref{eq:plant} with LQR gains).
Each region--controller pair was flown once ($N=1$) and recorded on video; the two \emph{plus}
missions also include three transit guards (stall detector, speed reduction above 16$^\circ$
tilt, 20~s start-wait timeout). A mission is complete when every surviving agent has finished its
lanes and returned to its cell centroid; a run is stopped if an agent that is not a scheduled
victim drops below 0.5~m. Metrics use the 20~Hz controller logs without the first 20~s and
without rows after a kill. Lateral error is taken during steady east--west sweeps (heading stable
for 1.5~s, speed above 0.6~m/s), torque effort is the mean of $\tau_x^2+\tau_y^2$, and obstacle
distance is measured from the drone centre to the cylinder surface; the airframe reaches
\BodyHalf~m (body) to \RotorReach~m (rotor tips), so smaller distances mean certain or possible
contact. We also assume a lightweight rotor guard, as on collision-tolerant multirotors
\cite{dePetrisRMFOwl2022}, so that grazing contacts load the guard rather than the propellers; the
guard is not simulated and changes no reported number.

\headingtwo{Mission Completion and Fault Recovery}
Table~\ref{tab:results} and Fig.~\ref{fig:traj} summarise the eight missions. Seven finished with
all survivors back at their cell centroids and \CovMin--\CovMax\% coverage, despite losing two of
seven agents at 18\% and 32\% coverage. Every obstacle map held all nine cylinders (centre and
radius within the 0.1~m log resolution) plus at most two unmatched tracks, and no completed
mission needed tier~3. Each kill causes a small drop, as the failed cell's coverage is erased,
before the slope resumes (Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}); Fig.~\ref{fig:env} shows the final maps. The
\LQR{} U-shape mission completed at \CovUshapeLqr\%, below the 97\% reference level of Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}, with thin uncovered strips along cell boundaries in
the left arm, nearly absent for \Hinf{}; their cause was not isolated. The \LQR{} rect mission
was stopped at \TmisRectLqr~s after an unscheduled crash (see Safety).

\headingtwo{Tracking under Turbulence: LQR versus $H_\infty$ Gains}
The two designs differ clearly in how tightly they hold a lane (Fig.~\ref{fig:tracking}a): the
median lateral error was \LatMedHinfMin--\LatMedHinfMax~cm with \Hinf{} and
\LatMedLqrMin--\LatMedLqrMax~cm with \LQR{}, and the RMS error fell from
\LatRmsLqrMin--\LatRmsLqrMax~cm to \LatRmsHinfMin--\LatRmsHinfMax~cm in all four regions
(\LatRedDoneMin--\LatRedDoneMax\% where both finished; \LatRedMax\% in the truncated rect pair). Most of the difference is a mean offset: \LQR{} flew $\LatMeanLqrMax$ to
$\LatMeanLqrMin$~cm downwind (towards $-y$), \Hinf{} within \LatMeanHinfMin--\LatMeanHinfMax~cm of
the lane, with closer standard deviations (\LatStdHinfMin--\LatStdHinfMax{} vs
\LatStdLqrMin--\LatStdLqrMax~cm). The $H_\infty$ outer integral gain is \KiRatio{} times larger,
and the \LQR{} node freezes its position integrator when the error to its set-point exceeds
0.25~m on either axis; since the set-point leads the vehicle along the lane by
$T_{\mathrm{lead}}u^\star\approx0.4$~m, this happened in \IntegOffLqrMin--\IntegOffLqrMax\% of its
sweep samples. Mean sweep speeds of
\VHinfMin--\VHinfMax{} against \VLqrMin--\VLqrMax~m/s (command 1.6~m/s) contribute to the
\TimeRedMin--\TimeRedMax\% shorter missions; the 95th-percentile tilt was essentially equal, as
was altitude error outside the two missions with upsets. In the linear analysis
(Table~\ref{tab:gains}) the $H_\infty$ gains lower the worst-case disturbance-to-position gain by
\HnormRatio{} times and the position deviation under the Gauss--Markov input by \SigxRatio{} times
(Fig.~\ref{fig:tracking}c).

The price is a higher effort: in the three completed pairs \Hinf{} used
\EffRatioMin--\EffRatioMax{} times the torque effort of \LQR{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:tracking}b), with motor saturation below 0.2\% of samples, and its outer
loop has a higher crossover (\WcHinf{} vs \WcLqr~rad/s) and smaller margins. The measured
reduction is far below the linear factor because tilt limit, pre-filter, QP rate limits and wind
force model are not in the linear model. As $Q$, $R$ differ and the integrator gating belongs to
the \LQR{} node, this compares two complete gain designs and does not isolate the effect of
$\gamma$ from that of higher loop gain.

\headingtwo{Safety: Clearance, Contact and Upset Events}
No two agents came closer than \VtwovLshapeLqr~m (\LQR{}, L-shape), above the 0.70~m hard
radius. The minimum surface distance to static cylinders was \ObssurfRectHinf,
\ObssurfLshapeHinf{} and \ObssurfUshapeHinf~m under \Hinf{} and \ObssurfRectLqr,
\ObssurfLshapeLqr{} and \ObssurfUshapeLqr~m under \LQR{} in rect, L- and U-shape, the \LQR{}
rect and L-shape values meaning body and possible rotor contact; in \emph{plus} both passed within
\ObssurfPlusHinf~m of the central cylinder, also inside the rotor envelope, where the assumed guard
takes the contact. The upsets (Fig.~\ref{fig:events}) share a pattern: each
happened while the agent stepped to its next lane beside a cylinder, after 
\TierSpanMin--\TierSpanMax~s of tier-2 solves bound by that cylinder. In the \LQR{} rect mission
agent 2 came within \UpRectLqrSurf~m of the cylinder at $(0,2.5)$ (body contact), tilted to
\UpRectLqrTilt$^\circ$ and fell; by protocol the run was then stopped, so this crash was scored as a
failed mission rather than handed to FT-CC. In \emph{plus}, agent 2 of both missions grazed the central
cylinder, tilting to \UpPlusLqrTilt$^\circ$ (\LQR) and \UpPlusHinfTilt$^\circ$ (\Hinf, dropping to
\UpPlusHinfZmin~m and recovering within about 3~s). With one run per case these events cannot be
attributed to the controller; plausible contributors are obstacle-blind lanes ending inside a
cylinder's margin, a barrier built on a 0.22~m disc, and a fixed 0.5~m/s$^2$ wind allowance that
ignores gusts of up to \WindMaxH~m/s. A \UpRectLqrSecondTilt$^\circ$ upset (\LQR{}, rect, agent 6,
\UpRectLqrSecondT~s) before sweeping, \UpRectLqrSecondSurf~m from the nearest cylinder, was
recovered. In all \NRunsHdynNeg{} missions some tier-2 solves bound by a moving-obstacle row
reported a negative minimum barrier value (down to $\HdynMinLogged$~m): the log keeps only the minimum over
all rows and no moving-cylinder positions, so contact can be neither confirmed nor excluded; from a
moving-obstacle row, that value would mean a surface distance of \MovSurfWorst~m, inside the rotor
envelope.

\headingtwo{Limitations}
The results rest on single runs (one wind seed), perfect state and moving-obstacle feedback, instant
failure detection, geometric coverage, unsimulated rotor guards and centralised upper layers; runs
are stopped at unscheduled crashes, and there is no ablation or re-implemented baseline.

% =====================================================================
\headingone{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================
A three-layer architecture of polygon-clipped Voronoi partitioning, boundary-following sweeps,
lane-level fault reallocation, a LiDAR-mapped actuator-consistent CBF-QP and Riccati-synthesised
PID control completed seven of eight simulated missions with \CovMin--\CovMax\% coverage under
turbulence, unmapped and moving obstacles, and the loss of two of seven quadrotors. Where both
controllers finished, $H_\infty$ gains held the lanes \LatRedDoneMin--\LatRedDoneMax\% more tightly
and shortened missions by \TimeRedMin--\TimeRedMax\% relative to LQR gains, at several times the
torque effort and with smaller margins. One LQR mission ended in an obstacle strike and three
others came within rotor reach of a cylinder. Next steps are runs over several wind
seeds, a guard-sized, gust-aware barrier, obstacle-aware lane ends, moving-obstacle logging,
distributed upper layers and flight tests.
